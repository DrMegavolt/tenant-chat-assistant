Presentation samples

Three styles, one deck.

The same six slides — title, agenda, cards, big stat, two-column, closing — rendered in three distinct visual styles, so you can compare treatments rather than topics. Each file is a single self-contained HTML file: no dependencies, no build step.

The decks

Dark Techstyle 01

Near-black canvas with a faint grid, one violet accent, mono kickers, terminal-style figure. Best for engineering, infrastructure and LLM/agent topics — reads well on a projector or screen-share in a dim room.
Editorial Lightstyle 02

Warm paper, serif display type, hairline rules, one signal-red accent. Best for talks that are read as much as watched — reviews, write-ups, anything that will also be printed or exported to PDF.
Gradient Keynotestyle 03

Indigo-to-cyan gradient field, frosted-glass cards, gradient display type. Best for product and vision keynotes where energy matters more than information density.

Good presentation style, in seven rules

  1. One idea per slide. The headline states it; everything else on the slide only proves or measures it.
  2. Type does the work. Three roles, three faces: a display face for headlines, a workhorse sans for prose, a mono for data. Headlines ≥ 40 px at 16:9.
  3. One accent color. Everything else is neutral. The accent marks exactly the thing you want eyes on — numbers, keywords, the total row.
  4. Margins and air. ≥ 7 vw padding, generous line spacing. A crowded slide reads as unfinished; whitespace reads as confidence.
  5. Data gets structure. Numbers in mono, columns aligned, units on every figure — the same discipline as a log line.
  6. Consistent chrome. Kicker, folio, footer and rules sit in the same place on every slide, so the audience never re-orients.
  7. Dark for the stage, light for the page. Dark decks flatter screens and dim rooms; light decks survive printing, PDFs and bright rooms. Pick by venue, not by mood.
